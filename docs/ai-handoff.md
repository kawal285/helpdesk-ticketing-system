# "I'm not sure" handoff (Needs human check)

An AI that always answers is less safe than one that knows when to stop
(selective prediction). So when the AI is unsure, it doesn't guess: the
ticket or answer is marked **Needs human check** and passed to a person,
and every handoff is recorded.

## Tickets

When a ticket is created, the AI suggests a category and a priority.
With the local Ollama model we also get the **probability the model gave
its own answer** (the first token after "Priority:" / "Category:"). A
ticket is marked "Needs human check" when:

| Reason | When |
| --- | --- |
| `low_priority_confidence` | The AI chose the priority with less than 50% probability |
| `low_category_confidence` | The AI chose the category with less than 50% probability |
| `unclear_ai_priority` / `unclear_ai_category` | The AI wrote something that isn't a real priority or category |
| `ai_unavailable` | The AI couldn't be reached, so defaults were used |
| `prompt_injection` | The ticket tried to give the AI instructions |
| `flagged_by_staff` | A staff member asked for a check by hand |

A value the customer picked themselves isn't the AI's guess, so its
confidence doesn't count. With Groq or the college API there are no token
probabilities, so tickets are never flagged for low confidence (unknown
is not treated as low).

Staff see a **Needs check** badge in the ticket lists, can filter with
"Needs human check only", and get a banner on the ticket with the reasons
and how sure the AI was. They clear it by changing the priority or
category, or with **Mark as checked**. The ticket records who checked it,
when, and whether they **kept** or **changed** the AI's answer.

The Report page shows the live numbers (`GET /tickets/handoff-summary`):
tickets handed to a person, still waiting, kept, changed, and the reasons.

### How the 50% cut-off was chosen

From the 40 hand-labelled tickets, run against the real model. Full
tables at every cut-off: [ai-handoff-evaluation.md](./ai-handoff-evaluation.md)
(regenerated by `node scripts/ai/evaluate-classification.js`).

At 50% for both fields:

- **7 of 40 tickets (17.5%) were handed to a person, and 6 of those 7
  really were wrong.** Only 1 was a needless handoff.
- Tickets the AI kept had both fields right 54.5% of the time, against
  47.5% with no handoff.
- **Category confidence is a strong signal**: all 4 category handoffs
  were real mistakes, and category accuracy on the rest rose to 91.7%.
- **Priority confidence is a weak signal for this small model**: it
  averages about 0.73 when right and 0.66 when wrong. Raising the cut-off
  catches more priority mistakes but hands off many correct tickets too
  (at 0.8, 70% of tickets go to a person and half of those were right).
  50% keeps the extra work small.

Limits: 40 tickets is a small set, and results move a few points between
runs (see [ai-classification-evaluation-report.md](./ai-classification-evaluation-report.md)).
The cut-offs can be changed with `AI_PRIORITY_CONFIDENCE_MIN` and
`AI_CATEGORY_CONFIDENCE_MIN` in `.env`. The live kept/changed numbers on
the Report page are the real check once staff start using it.

## AI assistant

The assistant only answers when the best knowledge-base match scores at
least 0.59. When the match **only just clears that line (below 0.64)**
and no web result backs it up, the answer is still shown, but with a
**Needs human check** note ("I'm not sure this matches your problem")
and the ticket button changes to "Ask a person (raise a ticket)".

Why 0.64: in the retrieval evaluation ([rag-evaluation.md](./rag-evaluation.md))
every out-of-scope question scored below 0.59, the closest at 0.581, and
correct matches started at 0.612 (a misspelled question). The band just
above the line is where an off-topic question would first slip through,
so answers there are shown as unsure rather than certain. The margin can
be changed with `RAG_LOW_CONFIDENCE_MARGIN` (default 0.05).

## Tests

`tests/humanHandoff.test.js` (25 tests): reading the token confidence,
each handoff reason, ignoring a customer-chosen field, unknown confidence
never counting as low, flagging on create, clearing by changing or
confirming (and recording which), customers can't clear it, staff can
flag by hand, the list filter, the handoff summary counts, and the
assistant's weak-match handling. `tests/classificationStats.test.js`
checks the handoff maths.
